Release 4.2 didn't lose callouts. It moved them away from four people, and the code gives them no way back.

The strongest explanation: the shorter answer window caused a burst of missed offers, and the code scores every miss like a "no." Nothing in the code lets a score heal, and four responders went from about 12 offers a week to almost none.

1 · Executive recommendation

4.2 is the most likely cause. Fix the score so it can heal, and test it in shadow before it goes live.

Don't wait for a "seasonal" bounce, and don't quietly change a number. Approve a 2-week shadow test of Back in Rotation, starting with score healing.

What I'm asking Helen to approve Recommendation

  1. Stop treating "seasonal" as the main explanation. Acceptance dropped 24 points in a single week, the week 4.2 shipped. The repo has no prior-year data that could support a seasonal pattern. Seasonality isn't disproven, but nothing points to it.
  2. Shadow-test score healing (about 2 weeks). Scores drift back toward the middle (0.5) over time. Log how rankings would change, but don't act on it yet.
  3. Set healing no faster than "halfway in 3 days." Faster healing starts rewarding people who refuse every job.
  4. Hold the "cheaper timeout" change until Ravi can split timeouts from deliberate "no"s.
  5. Decide separately whether to revisit the 60-second window. The evidence points to it as the likely trigger, though we can't yet prove the misses were timeouts. Back in Rotation deliberately leaves it alone.

How "contradicted" is defined, with all 25 tickets

Rule (re-checked for this deck): a ticket is contradicted if it says the responder got no offers, almost none, or a specific low number over some period, and every week of pings_sent covering that period shows 5 or more offers. It's partly contradicted if it says "nothing" but the covering weeks show 1–4 offers. It can't be checked if it only says an offer vanished, because the data has weekly totals, not per-offer timing.

Result: 18 contradicted, 3 partly, 4 can't check. This matches the Module 3 count. Limit: weeks start on Monday and tickets are filed mid-week, so a per-offer log could change individual calls (e.g. T-002 if all 10 of Ashgrove's offers came after 14 Aug). "Contradicted" doesn't mean the filer is wrong about what they saw on the phone; it means the data and the ticket disagree.

00-rook/feedback/tickets/t-001…t-025.txt vs 00-rook/data/callout-history.csv, re-checked ticket by ticket for this deck.

5 · Root cause

A feedback loop with no way out, in the code as written.

Click through the loop. Each step is either in the routing code (00-rook/code/dispatch-routing/) or in the data. That the steps linked up this way for the four is the best-supported explanation, not yet proven. Wen's score history and Ravi's per-offer log would confirm or refute it.

Confirmed by the code Confirmed

  • Timeout and "no" cost the same −0.12. history.py:35–39, offer.py:30
  • The clock starts when the offer is sent, not when it's seen. offer.py:41–42
  • No healing, decay or time window. history.py:30–34
  • The same rules apply to everyone, including existing decliners. This answers half of Marcus's 14 Aug question.
  • Nobody is removed; low scores just sink down the list. routing.py:21–22
  • No alerts or safety nets in this repo. Scores live only in memory. history.py:17

Ruled out or not supported

  • "It's seasonal" (not supported): the drop is a one-week step, not a slide, and there's no prior-year data to test it either way. Five analysts (30 Sept) agreed 4.2 was the more likely cause.
  • "They were already decliners": nobody was below 60% before 4.2, and pre-release acceptance barely predicts the loss (0.28).
  • "The weights alone did it" (unlikely): the new weights reduce the acceptance score's influence (0.40 → 0.25), which softens the penalty. A shorter window fits the one-week acceptance drop better. This comes from the Module 4 analysis, not a live test.

Still uncertain Open

  • Does the live system match the repo? (There's one commit.)
  • Did scores reset at the 4.2 deploy?
  • How many misses were timeouts vs. real "no"s?
  • Did phones actually show every offer?
  • Why Bulwark escaped and why Ashgrove/Halfmoon fell.
  • Whether the change was meant to hit existing decliners (the other half of Marcus's question).

Try it: the break-even point Confirmed arithmetic

+0.08 per yes (config.py:18), −0.12 per anything else (config.py:19). Break-even: 0.08 × a = 0.12 × (1 − a), so a = 60%.
6 · Proposed solution

Back in Rotation: let scores heal, without letting anyone jump the line.

I picked this direction (Approach 2) over giving quiet responders priority. Nobody gets put ahead of a closer or better-matched responder.

1 · Score healing Recommendation

A low score drifts back toward the middle (0.5), even with no offers. A deliberate "no" still costs the full 0.12. This is Wen's 2019 TODO, finally answered.

2 · Cheaper timeouts Pending data

A timeout costs less than a deliberate "no." The code already tells them apart (offer.py:14–16), but how much this helps depends on a split we don't have yet.

Fairness safeguards Recommendation

  • No line-jumping, guaranteed offers or tie-breaker.
  • "Ready now" only tells the handler; it never moves anyone up the list.
  • No change to the Availability Record that Supply reads.
  • No identity data is stored or inferred.

Replay: each person's score on 31 Aug under four rule sets

Real weekly history replayed through the code's rules. Assumption Everyone starts at 0.5 on 29 June. Half of misses are timeouts. A timeout costs half as much as a "no." Scores heal halfway in 5 days. This shows scores, not offers.

Healing does most of the work, because it keeps going after the offers stop. Ashgrove and Halfmoon sit at 1.00 under every rule, so their drop has a different cause.

Recomputed for this deck from callout-history.csv using the replay in 05-super-speed/prototype.html. It matches brief.md.

The Decliner Test: does healing reward refusals?

A made-up responder gets 10 offers a week and says "no" to all of them for 10 weeks. The bars show their average score when offered work. Simulated

Healing halfway in 1 day gives a full-time refuser 0.30. At 3 days or slower it stays near zero. Recommendation Heal no faster than 3 days, or pause healing after a deliberate "no."

A healed score is not offers back Confirmed arithmetic

1. Score gap: 1.0 − 0.5 = 0.5 (reliable peer vs. healed responder) 2. × acceptance weight 0.25 = 0.125 3. ÷ distance weight 0.60 = 0.208 4. × 45-minute horizon ≈ 9.4 minutes

A healed responder still ranks behind a reliable peer who is up to about 9 minutes farther away. config.py:13–14,26 · routing.py:38–54

Deliberately deferred Open

  • Where healing stops: at 0.5, or at a typical responder's score? Needs Wen's score data.
  • How big the timeout discount is: needs Ravi's timeout-vs-"no" split.
  • The healing speed: placeholder; 3 days or slower.
  • Ashgrove and Halfmoon: probably the 4.2 distance weighting, but unconfirmed.
  • Late phone delivery is out of scope here.

9 · Lessons learned

The best prompt from each module, and what it changed.

Quoted exactly from each module's prompts.md. Long prompts are cut with "…" (see the file for the full text).

Count people, not rows

Counting unique responders (Module 2), then checking every ticket against the data (Module 3), exposed 18 of 25 tickets that the data doesn't back up.

Ask what can't be proven

"What are we still assuming?" kept the score-reset and live-vs-repo questions open instead of quietly answered.

Make the tool check itself

"Verify rather than assume" caught that some citations came from notes, not from files opened that session.
