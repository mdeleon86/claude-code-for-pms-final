How this simulation works (assumptions)
  • Starting point is real: Mite got 1 offer the week of 31 Aug. Since the release week he accepted 5 of 17 offers and missed 12, which would sink his track record to the floor, so the simulation starts it at 0.
  • Today's rules are from the code (config.py, history.py): +0.08 for accepting, −0.12 for a "no" or a timeout, kept between 0 and 1, and no healing.
  • Back in Rotation values are placeholders for Wen to decide: a timeout costs −0.06 (half of today), a "no" still costs −0.12, and a low score heals halfway back to 0.5 every 10 / 5 / 3 days (slow / medium / fast).
  • The biggest assumption, offers vs. track record: at 0 he gets about 1 offer a week (as in late Aug), and at 0.5 or above about 11 (his usual before 4.2), in a straight line between. The real relationship depends on distance and other responders, which we don't have.
  • How Mite responds: "Like before 4.2" uses his real pre-release acceptance (46 of 67 = 69%), so 70% accept, 20% time out, 10% decline. "Keeps timing out": 40% / 50% / 10%. "Mostly misses": 20% / 70% / 10%.
  • Averages, not chance. Each week uses expected numbers, so the same settings always give the same result. "Reaches 10+ offers a week" depends on the score-to-offers assumption above. It isn't a promise.

Open design question (not decided)

Healing stops at the middle (0.5). Responders who say yes often score near 1.0, so even "both changes" leaves the four at about 0.45, still behind them. Should healing stop at 0.5, or aim for a typical responder's score? Aiming higher brings people back faster, but makes it easier for someone who refuses work to bounce back. We need Wen's real score data before deciding.

2. The Decliner Test

Does healing reward someone who keeps saying "no" on purpose? This made-up responder gets 10 offers every week and says "no" to every one, for 10 weeks. For comparison, a reliable responder gets the same 10 offers and says yes to 7.

This is a stress test. It keeps sending 10 offers a week even as the score drops, which real routing wouldn't do. The scores shown are the ones the decliner has at the moment each offer arrives.

What's real and what's assumed in the Rule lab
  • Real: each responder's weekly offers and "yes" answers, 29 Jun–31 Aug, from callout-history.csv. Nothing is filled in. Farlight really got 0 offers in the week of 31 Aug.
  • Real rules (from the code): +0.08 for yes, −0.12 for anything else, a score between 0 and 1, and new responders start at 0.5 (config.py, history.py, offer.py).
  • Not in the data: whether each miss was a timeout or a real "no." Slider 1 is a guess, applied the same way to everyone.
  • Assumed: everyone starts at 0.5 on 29 Jun, because their real score isn't recorded. Inside each week, offers are spread evenly across the 7 days, with yes answers spread evenly among them. Healing happens a little each day.
  • The replay keeps their real offers fixed. It shows how each rule would have scored the same history. It doesn't show how many offers they would have got with a higher score, and it isn't a prediction of real dispatch results.
  • Placeholder values: the healing speed and the timeout discount are still Wen's decision.

What's real and what's made up

  • Real data: Meteor Mite's and The Gale's weekly offers (29 Jun–31 Aug) come from 00-rook/data/callout-history.csv.
  • Proposed, not built: scores healing over time toward neutral, and a timeout costing less than a deliberate "no." Speeds and amounts are still Wen's call.
  • Calculated, using placeholder rules: Mite's score on the console and phone. It starts at 0 on day 0, heals halfway back toward 0.5 every 5 days, and changes with your clicks: +0.08 for accept, −0.12 for decline, −0.06 for a timeout. It never goes above the middle (0.5) by healing alone.
  • Illustrative: whether offers come back at +14 days (the two scenarios), The Gale easing, the 11-minute incident, and every line after 31 Aug in Simulation A.
  • Response time: only a timeout holds a job up (up to 60 seconds). A deliberate "no" passes it on right away.
  • "Ready now" is a heads-up only. It tells Kip that Mite is by the phone. It never moves Mite up the list.
  • Open question: the "gone quiet" flag depends on what pings_sent counts. That needs confirming with Ravi before this could be built.
